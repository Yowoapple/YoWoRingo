Origin

"Can I add youon Discord?"

In 2018 I was watching an earthquake livestream and asked another person in the chat, someone who was also recording earthquakes, whether we could keep talking on Discord. That question became TWERG.

It grew into the earliest long-running earth science community on Discord in the Chinese-speaking world: earthquakes, typhoons, heavy rain and everything in between, shared as fast and as accurately as we can.

    The community's home: live earthquake reports, a notable-earthquake archive, a changelog and policies. Built by me, in four languages.

  2. 02 / DYFI

    Did You Feel It?

    A felt-report system that turns what people feel into a town-level intensity map, checked against instruments. More on it below.

Deep dive

DYFI,done properly.

A shaking map built from people instead of sensors. Getting it right meant reading the research, and then finding where a naive version goes wrong.

01 / Foundation

Built on USGS "Did You Feel It?"

The method follows the USGS framework for community decimal intensity, then maps the result onto the Central Weather Administration's own intensity scale so it speaks the same language as official reports.

02 / The mistake I fixed

Never average people.

The obvious approach, scoring each person and averaging the scores, quietly breaks: someone who was outdoors cannot answer the indoor questions, and a naive average drags the whole area down. Following Quitoriano and Wald, the system first finds the community's consensus on each question, then computes one intensity for the area.

03 / The questions

Three kinds of evidence

What people felt, what moved indoors and what happened outside are weighed as separate factors, with an anchor question that grounds the rest.

04 / Place

Town by town

Reports are located to their town, grouped on a grid and cleaned of outliers. "I didn't feel it" is kept too, because it marks where the shaking stopped.

05 / Trust

Checked against instruments

Every area is compared with nearby seismic stations and with an intensity prediction model, so reports that do not fit the physics are flagged.

06 / Privacy

Nothing personal kept

Abuse protection stores only a one-way hash of an address, deleted automatically within about two days.
